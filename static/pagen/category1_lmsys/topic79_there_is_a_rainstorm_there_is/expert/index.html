Raindrops in Thunderstorms The Hidden Physics of Water Droplets

As you watch a snake slither past in the storm, the raindrops on its scales aren't teardrop-shaped at all. In lightning flashes, you'd see them as rounded or flattened spheres — but falling so fast, they appear as streaks to the naked eye.

Quick Answer

Raindrops are not teardrop-shaped — that's a myth from cartoons. Small raindrops stay nearly spherical because of surface tension, while larger ones flatten at the bottom and bulge at the sides, looking more like a hamburger bun. In a thunderstorm, with heavy rain and strong winds, you'd actually see them as blurred streaks, not distinct shapes, because they fall so fast past your eyes.

Surface Tension

Water molecules are held together by hydrogen bonds, creating surface tension that initially forms spherical droplets. This is why small raindrops start as perfect spheres.

Air Resistance

As raindrops fall, air resistance flattens the bottom while the top remains curved, creating the characteristic "hamburger bun" shape rather than the tear shape we imagine.

Forces in Balance

When raindrops exceed 4mm in diameter, surface tension can no longer hold them together, causing them to break apart into smaller droplets.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about raindrops, thunderstorms, and the fascinating physics behind them.

Why aren't raindrops tear-shaped like we see in cartoons?

Contrary to popular belief, raindrops are not tear-shaped! They start as spherical droplets due to surface tension, but as they fall through the air, air resistance flattens the bottom while the top remains curved, creating a "hamburger bun" shape. Only very small droplets (less than 1mm) remain nearly spherical.

If raindrops aren't tear-shaped, why do we think they are?

Because raindrops move quickly, our eyes see them as streaks, and art/cartoons simplified this into a teardrop symbol. Real raindrops are nearly spherical or bun-shaped, but motion blur and artistic convention have created the teardrop myth.

What's the largest raindrop ever recorded?

The largest raindrops typically reach about 6-8mm in diameter before breaking apart due to air resistance. Drops larger than 4mm become unstable and split into smaller droplets. The theoretical maximum is around 8mm, but most raindrops are much smaller, averaging 1-2mm.

How fast do raindrops fall?

Raindrop speed depends on size, but most fall at 6-9 meters per second (13-20 mph). Small drops (1mm) fall at about 4 m/s, while large drops (5mm) can reach 9 m/s. This is their "terminal velocity" - the maximum speed where air resistance balances gravity.

Why do snakes come out during rain?

Snakes become more active during rain for several reasons: their underground dens may flood, forcing them to seek higher ground; their prey (frogs, rodents) are also more active; the moisture helps with skin shedding; and they may be hunting for food that's easier to catch in wet conditions.

How does lightning form in thunderstorms?

Lightning forms when ice particles and water droplets collide in the storm cloud, creating electrical charges. Positive charges accumulate at the top of the cloud, negative charges at the bottom. When the electrical potential difference becomes too great (millions of volts), lightning discharges occur, heating the air to 30,000°C.

Can you really tell how far away lightning is by counting seconds?

Yes! Since light travels much faster than sound, you can estimate lightning distance by counting seconds between the flash and thunder. Sound travels at about 343 m/s (1,125 ft/s), so divide the seconds by 3 to get approximate distance in kilometers, or by 5 for miles. For example, 3 seconds = about 1 km away.

Why do some raindrops feel harder than others?

The "hardness" you feel depends on raindrop size and speed. Larger drops (3-5mm) have more mass and hit with greater force. Wind can also accelerate drops horizontally, making them feel harder. Temperature affects this too - colder drops are denser and feel more solid.

What causes the different colors in rainbows?

Rainbows form when sunlight enters raindrops and gets refracted (bent) and internally reflected. Different wavelengths of light bend at slightly different angles, separating white light into its component colors. The size and shape of raindrops affect the rainbow's appearance - larger, more spherical drops create brighter, more distinct rainbows.

Why does rain sometimes smell?

The distinctive "rain smell" is called petrichor, caused by plant oils and soil bacteria released into the air. Ozone from lightning also contributes to the fresh, clean scent we associate with thunderstorms.

Can thunderstorms cause tornadoes?

Yes! Severe thunderstorms with strong wind shear can develop into supercells, which are the primary producers of tornadoes. The rotating updrafts in supercells create the perfect conditions for tornado formation.

Why do double rainbows form?

Double rainbows occur when light undergoes two internal reflections inside raindrops instead of one. The secondary rainbow appears above the primary one with reversed colors (red on the inside, violet on the outside) and is typically dimmer.

How long does a typical thunderstorm last?

Most thunderstorms last 30 minutes to 1 hour, but severe storms can persist much longer. Supercell thunderstorms can last several hours, while multicell systems may continue for many hours as new cells develop and replace old ones.
