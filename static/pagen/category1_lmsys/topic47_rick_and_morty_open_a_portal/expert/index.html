Portal to Reality: A Message About Writing

They're Here. They Want Better Writing Everywhere.

In an unprecedented event, Rick and Morty have successfully opened a portal from their dimension and entered our reality. After traveling across infinite dimensions and observing the state of writing across all media—television, film, books, games, and more—they've appeared with one clear message: "Writers everywhere, try harder."

Having seen countless realities and dimensions, Rick and Morty have identified universal problems that plague storytelling everywhere. They've opened this portal to our world specifically to deliver their comprehensive analysis of what's wrong with modern writing and what needs to change. What follows is their direct message to writers across all forms of media.

The Problems They've Seen

📺
Television & Streaming
Endless reboots, formulaic plots, and character resets. Shows that start strong but lose their way. Finales that disappoint. The same patterns repeat across infinite dimensions.
🎬
Film Industry
Sequels that exist only to make money. Remakes that add nothing new. Original ideas getting buried. Plot holes that break immersion. Stories that are just going through the motions without any real purpose or passion.
📚
Books & Literature
Stories that talk down to readers. Characters that never grow. Endings that feel rushed or unsatisfying. Even in the worst dimensions, some books manage to respect their audience—why can't more do the same?
🎮
Video Games
Stories that feel like afterthoughts. Dialogue that's just exposition. Characters that are walking plot devices. Games that could be amazing but settle for "good enough." In some dimensions, games are treated as a primary art form with storytelling that rivals any medium.
📱
Digital Content
Content created for algorithms, not audiences. Clickbait narratives. Stories designed to go viral rather than to matter. Quantity over quality. In infinite realities, the best content still comes from caring about the story, not the metrics.
🌐
Universal Issues
Across all dimensions and all media, the same problems persist: lack of originality, fear of taking risks, prioritizing profit over art, and treating audiences like they're stupid. These aren't dimension-specific—they're writer-specific.

Why Writing Quality Matters

Poor writing doesn't just disappoint audiences—it has real consequences. Across dimensions, the impact of bad writing affects everything from individual experiences to entire industries. Here's why this matters:

😔
Audience Disappointment
When stories fail, audiences feel it. They invest time, emotion, and money into content that lets them down. Poor writing creates cynicism, distrust, and eventually, disengagement. People stop caring because they've been burned too many times.
💼
Industry Decline
When quality drops, audiences leave. Streaming services lose subscribers. Movie theaters empty. Book sales decline. The industry suffers when writers prioritize profit over craft. Short-term gains lead to long-term losses.
🎨
Creative Stagnation
Formulaic writing kills innovation. When everyone follows the same patterns, creativity dies. New voices get silenced. Original ideas get buried. The art form stops evolving, and we're left with endless remakes and reboots.
🧠
Cultural Impact
Stories shape culture. They influence how we think, what we value, and who we become. When writing is lazy, it reflects and reinforces lazy thinking. Good writing challenges us, makes us grow, and helps us understand the world better.
👥
Wasted Potential
So many stories have incredible potential but fail because of poor execution. Great concepts get ruined by bad dialogue. Interesting characters get wasted on weak plots. The talent is there—the execution isn't.
🌍
Lost Opportunities
In dimensions where writers actually try, stories change lives. They inspire people. They create movements. They become part of cultural history. Here? Too many stories are forgotten the moment they end because they never mattered in the first place.

"The difference between good writing and bad writing isn't talent—it's effort."

- Rick

What Writers Everywhere Need to Do

1

Character Development That Actually Matters

"Stop resetting character growth. If a character learns something, let them keep that knowledge. Character arcs should build, not reset. I've seen countless dimensions, and even the worst ones have better continuity than most shows, movies, and books here." - Rick

2

Plot Consistency and Logic

"I can accept interdimensional travel, magic, or whatever your world has. But I can't accept plot holes that make no sense even in that context. If you establish rules, follow them. If you break them, explain why. Consistency matters across all genres." - Rick

3

Meaningful Dialogue

"Stop using dialogue just to move the plot forward. Give characters actual voices, actual personalities. Dialogue should reveal character, create conflict, and feel real—not like exposition dumps or catchphrase factories." - Morty

4

Creative Storytelling & Originality

"Stop remaking the same stories. Stop following formulas. Try something new. Experiment. Take risks. That's what I do in every dimension, and it works. Originality isn't dead—lazy writing killed it." - Rick

5

Respect the Audience's Intelligence

"Stop dumbing things down. Your audience is smart. They can handle complex themes, philosophical questions, moral ambiguity, and emotional depth. Write like you respect them. Treat them like adults, even if your content is for kids." - Rick

6

Show, Don't Tell

"Instead of having characters explain everything, let actions and situations speak. Visual storytelling. Subtext. Layers. Trust your audience to figure things out. You know, actual writing craft instead of lazy exposition." - Rick

7

Emotional Depth and Character Relationships

"Characters aren't just plot devices. Give them real relationships, real emotions, real consequences. Even in infinite dimensions, relationships matter. Write like you care about the characters as people, not just tools to move the story forward." - Morty

8

Proper Pacing and Structure

"Stop rushing to the end. Stop dragging out the middle. Find the right pace for your story. Some stories need time to breathe. Others need to move fast. But all of them need structure that serves the story, not the runtime or page count." - Rick

9

Meaningful Endings

"Stop writing endings that feel rushed, unsatisfying, or like you gave up. Endings matter. They're what people remember. Whether it's happy, sad, or ambiguous, make it feel earned. Make it feel like it matters." - Morty
