The Legend of 420

How five high schoolers accidentally created a global phenomenon

1971

The Birth of 420

At San Rafael High School in California, five friends known as "The Waldos" had a secret mission. They'd meet at 4:20 PM after school to search for a legendary abandoned cannabis crop hidden somewhere in the Point Reyes forest.

"We got tired of the Friday night football scene with all of the jocks. We were the guys sitting under the stands smoking a doobie, wondering what we were doing there." - Dave Reddix, The Waldos
1970s

The Secret Code

They'd pass each other in the hallways and whisper "420-Louis" (referring to the Louis Pasteur statue where they met). The "Louis" part eventually faded, but "420" stuck around as their secret signal.

420-Louis → 420
Late 1970s

Grateful Dead Connection

One of the Waldos had a family member working with the Grateful Dead. Through band rehearsals, road trips, and Deadhead communities, the term "420" began spreading beyond San Rafael. The counterculture movement embraced this secret code.

High Times Popularizes

High Times magazine discovered and published the 420 flyer, catapulting the term from underground circles to mainstream awareness. This media exposure transformed 420 from a secret code into a global cultural phenomenon.
