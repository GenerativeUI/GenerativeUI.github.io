Why Plants Are Green

Chlorophyll absorbs blue (435nm) and red (670nm) light but reflects green—making plants appear green. Explore the spectrum below.

400nm 500nm 600nm 700nm

Dark areas = absorbed by chlorophyll. Green (500–600nm) is reflected.

550 nm
Low absorption — reflected (green light)
Chloroplast · Thylakoids

Photosynthesis occurs in chloroplasts. Light reactions in thylakoids produce ATP & NADPH; dark reactions in stroma fix CO₂ into glucose.

The Photosynthesis Equation

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Carbon dioxide + water + sunlight produce glucose (sugar) + oxygen. Chlorophyll-a has a magnesium ion at its center—essential for capturing light. Plants also use carotenoids (orange/yellow) and anthocyanins (red/purple) for additional light absorption.

Why Green Feels Good

Green sits at 555nm—the wavelength our eyes are most sensitive to. It's the most restful color for the human eye and dominates nature.

Green Living Tips

Click the info icon for more details. Small changes add up—global resource use could rise 60% by 2060 without action.

🚴
Transport
Switch one car trip per week to walking, cycling, or transit.
Saves ~260 kg CO₂/year. 80% of global energy still comes from fossil fuels—every trip counts.
💡
Energy
Switch to LED bulbs and unplug devices when not in use.
LEDs use 75% less energy. Phantom drain from standby devices adds ~5–10% to bills.
🥗
Diet
Reduce meat, especially beef. One meat-free day = big impact.
Beef produces ~27 kg CO₂ per kg. Plant-based proteins are 10–50× lower. Vegan diet ≈ 1,500 kg CO₂/year less than high-meat.
♻️
Waste
Compost food scraps. Avoid single-use plastics.
Food waste in landfills produces methane. Composting returns nutrients to soil. Reusable bags save ~500 bags/year per person.
🌱
Plants
Add indoor plants—they filter air and boost wellbeing.
Plants may remove some VOCs in lab conditions, but real-world impact is minimal. They still boost wellbeing and add life to your space. Snake plants and pothos are low-maintenance—start with one and expand.
🛒
Shopping
Buy local, seasonal produce. Choose products with FSC, Organic, or Fair Trade labels.
Local food reduces transport emissions. Seasonal produce needs less energy (no heated greenhouses). Certifications ensure environmental and social standards.
🏠
Home
Insulate, use programmable thermostats, and fix leaks.
Heating/cooling is ~50% of home energy. Lower thermostat 1°C in winter = ~7% savings. Seal drafts and insulate attics for big impact.
🌳
Green Spaces
Spend time in parks and nature. Support urban tree planting.
Forest bathing reduces cortisol. Urban trees cool cities, filter air, and absorb CO₂. One mature tree absorbs ~22 kg CO₂/year.

Green Glossary

Key terms and definitions for green living and sustainability.

Chlorophyll
Green pigment in chloroplasts that absorbs light for photosynthesis. Contains magnesium. Reflects green (500–600nm), absorbs blue and red.
Photosynthesis
Process by which plants convert CO₂ and water into glucose using sunlight. Occurs in chloroplasts. Produces oxygen as byproduct.
Carbon Footprint
Total greenhouse gas emissions caused by an individual, organization, or product. Measured in kg or tons of CO₂ equivalent per year.
Biodiversity
Variety of life—species, ecosystems, genetic diversity. Essential for ecosystem health. WWF reports 73% wildlife decline since 1970.
Circular Economy
Economic model that eliminates waste: reuse, repair, recycle. Contrasts with linear "take-make-dispose" model.
VOCs
Volatile Organic Compounds—gases from paints, cleaners, furniture. Lab studies showed some plants can remove them in sealed chambers; real-world impact is minimal.
PAR
Photosynthetically Active Radiation—light wavelengths 400–700nm that plants use for photosynthesis.
Wishcycling
Putting unrecyclable items in recycling bins hoping they'll be recycled. Contaminates batches; check local rules.
Thylakoid
Membrane-bound compartments inside chloroplasts where light reactions of photosynthesis occur. Stack into grana.
Stroma
Fluid filling chloroplasts. Site of the Calvin cycle (dark reactions) where CO₂ is fixed into glucose.

Green Certifications

Look for these labels when shopping for sustainable products.

🌲
FSC
Forest Stewardship Council—paper and wood from responsibly managed forests. Chain-of-custody traced.
🤝
Fair Trade
Guarantees minimum prices for producers, safe conditions, no child labor. Covers coffee, cocoa, produce, and more.
B
B Corp
Companies meeting high social and environmental standards. B Impact Assessment score 80+. Recertified every 3 years.
🌿
Organic
No synthetic pesticides or fertilizers. Soil health, biodiversity, and animal welfare standards. USDA, EU, or national bodies certify.

Green Tech

Renewable energy and efficiency are key to decarbonization. Solar and wind are now the cheapest new electricity in most regions.

☀️
Solar PV
Costs dropped 90% since 2010. Rooftop solar pays back in 5–10 years in sunny regions.
💨
Wind
Onshore wind is among the cheapest electricity sources. Offshore expanding rapidly.
🔋
EVs
Electric vehicles emit less over lifecycle than ICE. Grid decarbonization improves this further.
🏠
Heat Pumps
3–4× more efficient than gas furnaces. Key for building decarbonization.

Planet-Friendly Green Foods

Low-carbon, nutrient-dense options. Plant-based foods typically have 10–50× lower emissions than animal products.

🥬
Leafy Greens
Kale, spinach, lettuce—very low footprint. Grow easily in gardens. Rich in iron, vitamins.
🥦
Broccoli
~0.5 kg CO₂ per kg. High in fiber, vitamin C. Choose local and seasonal.
🥒
Cucumbers
Low emissions. Hydrating. Avoid plastic wrap—buy loose when possible.
🥑
Avocados
Moderate footprint (water-intensive). Still far lower than cheese or beef. Source responsibly.
🫛
Peas & Legumes
Lentils, chickpeas, peas fix nitrogen in soil. Protein-rich, very low emissions.
🍃
Zucchini
Very low footprint. Prolific in home gardens. Versatile—raw, grilled, or baked.
🌿
Green Beans
Low emissions, good protein. Buy fresh and local when in season. Freeze surplus.
