Since September 4, 1998

Google

Larry Page and Sergey Brin met at Stanford in 1995. Their research produced "The Anatomy of a Large-Scale Hypertextual Web Search Engine" (1998) and the PageRank algorithm—ranking pages by backlinks, not just keywords. The name "Google" is a play on "googol"—the number 10100.

Explore the Googol

1010 = 10,000,000,000

A googol (10¹⁰⁰) is larger than the number of atoms in the observable universe (~10⁸⁰). Google's name reflects its mission to organize an unfathomable amount of information.

2004 IPO — Dutch Auction

Google went public August 19, 2004, using a modified Dutch auction—unconventional on Wall Street. Offered 19.6M shares at $85–95; closed first day at $100.30. The founders wanted transparency and to let ordinary users participate, not just institutional investors who would flip shares.

"Don't Be Evil"

Google's original motto appeared in the 2004 IPO founders' letter. It reflected the casual, value-driven culture shaped by Page and Brin. The Dutch auction itself embodied this—avoiding the early "pop" that enriched short-term traders.

Milestone Timeline

Key moments that shaped the company from a garage startup to a global technology leader

1996

BackRub Begins

Larry Page and Sergey Brin develop "BackRub" at Stanford—a search engine that ranks pages by analyzing backlinks. This link-analysis approach, published in "The Anatomy of a Large-Scale Hypertextual Web Search Engine" (1998), would become the foundation of Google. The initial database indexed at least 24 million pages.

1997

Google.com Registered

The domain google.com is registered on September 15. The name "Google" is a misspelling of "googol" (10¹⁰⁰), reflecting the mission to organize the world's information. The first logo was created by Sergey Brin using GIMP.

1998

Incorporation & First Doodle

Sun co-founder Andy Bechtolsheim writes a $100,000 check after a brief demo. Google Inc. incorporates September 4 in Susan Wojcicki's Menlo Park garage. The first Google Doodle—a Burning Man stick figure—appears August 30, making Doodles older than Google Inc. itself.

1999

Move to Mountain View

With $25M from Kleiner Perkins and Sequoia Capital, Google grows to 8 employees and moves to Mountain View. Ruth Kedar designs the new logo using the Catull font. "The PageRank Citation Ranking: Bringing Order to the Web" paper is published with Rajeev Motwani and Terry Winograd.

2002

Google News

Google News launches September 22, aggregating headlines from ~4,000 sources. Created by Krishna Bharat after 9/11 to help find news from multiple viewpoints. The automated service would become a major traffic source for publishers.

2004

Gmail, Keyhole & IPO

Gmail launches April 1 with 1GB free storage—100x more than Yahoo and Hotmail. Many thought it was an April Fool's joke; invitations later sold on eBay for over $100. Google acquires Keyhole Corp (foundation for Google Earth). Google goes public August 19 with a Dutch auction IPO—$85–95/share, closed first day at $100.30. The motto "Don't Be Evil" was in the IPO letter.

2005

Google Maps & Android

Google Maps launches in February with satellite imagery. Google acquires Android Inc. for ~$50 million—a 22-month-old startup that would power over 70% of the world's smartphones. Google.org, the charitable arm, is established.

2006

YouTube Acquisition

Google acquires YouTube for $1.65 billion when it had only 67 employees. CEO Eric Schmidt called it "the next step in the evolution of the Internet." Google beat bids from Microsoft, Yahoo, and News Corp.

2007

Street View & DoubleClick

Street View launches May 25 in five U.S. cities (San Francisco, Miami, Denver, Las Vegas, New York). Technology came from the Stanford CityBlock Project. Google acquires DoubleClick for $3.1 billion, expanding its advertising platform.

2008

Chrome Browser

Google Chrome launches September 2, introducing the V8 JavaScript engine and a minimalist design. It would eventually become the world's most used browser. Android 1.0 ships on the T-Mobile G1.

2009

Self-Driving Car Project

Google's Self-Driving Car Project launches officially (roots in Stanford's 2004 team). This would evolve into Waymo, a leader in autonomous vehicle technology. Gmail exits beta after 5 years.

2011

Panda & Motorola

Panda algorithm update targets low-quality content (affecting ~12% of queries). Google acquires Motorola Mobility for $12.5 billion—primarily for 20,000+ mobile patents. Later sold to Lenovo for ~$2.9B in 2014.

2012

Penguin & Google Drive

Penguin algorithm combats webspam and manipulative link building (~3.1% of queries affected). Google Drive launches April 24 with 5GB free storage, integrated with Docs, Sheets, and Slides.

2013

Hummingbird & Waze

Hummingbird algorithm improves semantic search and conversational queries. Google acquires Waze for $1.3 billion, enhancing Maps with real-time traffic from community reports.

2014

DeepMind & Nest

Google acquires DeepMind for ~$400 million—British AI company specializing in machine learning (later created AlphaGo). Nest acquired for $3.2 billion, entering the smart home market.

2015

Alphabet, RankBrain & Logo

Google restructures as a subsidiary of Alphabet Inc.; Larry Page becomes Alphabet CEO, Sundar Pichai becomes Google CEO. RankBrain is announced—Google's third-most-important ranking factor, using ML for ~15% of queries. New flat Product Sans logo debuts September 1.

2016

Waymo Spinoff

Google's Self-Driving Car Project spins off as Waymo on December 13, 2016—an independent company under Alphabet. John Krafcik becomes CEO. Waymo had logged 2M+ miles of road testing. Would later launch commercial autonomous ride-hailing.

2024

AI & Search Evolution

Google integrates AI Overviews into Search. The company processes billions of searches daily (estimates range from 8B to 20B+). Market share remains ~90% globally. Google makes 600+ algorithm updates per year—about 13 per day.

Search Algorithm Evolution

Google makes 600+ algorithm updates per year—about 13 per day. These are the major named updates that reshaped search.

Panda — February 2011

Targeted low-quality content: thin content, content farms, keyword stuffing, high ad-to-content ratios. Affected ~12% of search queries. Required sites to audit and remove low-value pages.

Penguin — April 2012

Combated webspam and manipulative link building. Penalized unnatural links, low-quality backlinks, exact-match anchor overuse, link schemes. Initial rollout affected ~3.1% of queries.

Hummingbird — August 2013

Focused on semantic search and conversational queries. Improved understanding of query meaning rather than keyword matching. Better handling of long-tail and natural language searches.

RankBrain — October 2015

Machine learning algorithm; Google's third-most-important ranking factor. Handles unfamiliar queries by guessing similar meanings. Used for <15% of queries initially but predicted top results 80% of the time vs. 70% for human engineers.

AI Contributions

From TensorFlow to Gemini—Google's research and products have shaped modern artificial intelligence

TensorFlow — November 2015

Open-source machine learning library from Google's Brain Team. Data-flow graphs for numerical computation; runs on CPU, GPU, TPU. Apache 2.0 licensed. Reached 1.0 in February 2017. Foundation for much of modern ML research and production.

BERT — 2018

Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers. Pre-trained language model achieving state-of-the-art on 11 NLP tasks. Released open source; influenced GPT and the transformer era. Published at NAACL 2019.

AlphaGo — 2016

DeepMind's AI mastered Go and defeated world champion Lee Sedol. Demonstrated AI tackling strategic challenges requiring human-like intuition. Inspired a new era of reinforcement learning and game-playing AI.

AlphaFold — 2020+

Solves the protein-folding problem—predicting 3D structure from amino acid sequences. AlphaFold 3 predicts protein interactions with other molecules. Over 200 million structures in the free database. Transformative for drug design and biology.

Gemini & Generative AI

Bard launched March 2023 (LaMDA, then PaLM 2). Rebranded as Gemini December 2023 with Nano, Pro, Ultra. Gemini 2.0 (2024) designed for the "agentic era." Gemini 2.5 Pro and Flash (2025). Deep Research generates reports with citations. Integrated across Search, Workspace, Vertex AI.

Veo 2 & Imagen 3 — 2024

Veo 2: Video generation up to 4K, minutes long; understands physics, cinematography. Imagen 3: State-of-the-art image generation, diverse styles. Available via VideoFX, ImageFX, Vertex AI.

MusicFX

Generative music up to 70 seconds. 2024 update: higher-quality audio, faster generation. Music preferred 3× over previous versions in testing.

Gemma

Open models (2B, 7B) from Gemini research. Lightweight, state-of-the-art. Continues Google's open-source tradition with TensorFlow, BERT, AlphaFold.

Research & Emerging

SIMA 2

Agent that plays, reasons, and learns in virtual 3D worlds. Trained on video game environments.

Gemini Robotics

World models for robotic understanding. Physical agents that perceive and act in real environments.

Game Arena

DeepMind benchmark platform. Beyond chess—Werewolf, poker—testing social dynamics and risk management in AI.

The First Google Doodle

August 30, 1998—a stick figure that started a global tradition

Burning Man, August 30, 1998

Larry Page and Sergey Brin were heading to the Burning Man festival in Nevada. As an out-of-office message, they replaced the second "o" in Google with a stick figure—the festival's symbol. The first Doodle was posted less than a week before Google officially incorporated on September 4, making Doodles older than Google Inc. itself.

"It was a little bit of a joke," says Jessica Yu, Google Doodle team lead. The remarkably simple design established a concept that would evolve significantly. Google didn't create another Doodle for two years. In 2000, Dennis Hwang—then an intern—designed the Bastille Day (July 14) doodle. Hwang became chief Doodle designer, creating about 50 per year and establishing the program that now celebrates holidays, historical figures, and notable events worldwide.
