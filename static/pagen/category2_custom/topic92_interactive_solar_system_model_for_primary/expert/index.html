Welcome to Our Solar System

The solar system is our cosmic neighborhood, consisting of the Sun and everything that orbits around it. This includes eight planets, their moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, comets, and countless smaller objects. Our solar system formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a giant cloud of gas and dust.

The Sun sits at the center of our solar system and contains 99.8% of all the mass in the solar system. Its gravity keeps all the planets in orbit. The planets closer to the Sun are rocky and smaller, while the outer planets are mostly made of gas and are much larger.

The Eight Planets

Our solar system has eight planets that orbit the Sun. They are divided into two groups: the inner rocky planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and the outer gas giants (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune).

Inner Rocky Planets

Mercury

The smallest and fastest planet, Mercury has no atmosphere and experiences extreme temperature changes. During the day, it can reach 430°C, but at night it drops to -180°C. Its surface is covered with craters from asteroid impacts.

Venus

Often called Earth's twin due to similar size, Venus has a thick, toxic atmosphere that traps heat. It's the hottest planet with surface temperatures around 465°C. Venus rotates backwards compared to most planets.

Earth

Our home planet is the only known world with liquid water on its surface and life. Earth has one moon and a protective atmosphere that shields us from harmful space radiation. About 71% of Earth's surface is covered by water.

Mars

The Red Planet gets its color from iron oxide (rust) in its soil. Mars has the largest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons, and ice caps at its poles. Scientists are studying Mars for signs of ancient life.

Jupiter

The largest planet in our solar system, Jupiter is so big that all other planets could fit inside it. It has a famous storm called the Great Red Spot that has lasted for hundreds of years. Jupiter has at least 95 moons.

Saturn

Known for its spectacular ring system made of ice and rock particles, Saturn is the second-largest planet. It's less dense than water, which means it would float if you could find a bathtub big enough! Saturn has 146 confirmed moons.

Uranus

This ice giant rotates on its side, making it unique among planets. Uranus has a blue-green color due to methane in its atmosphere. It's very cold, with temperatures dropping to -224°C. Uranus has 28 known moons.

Neptune

The most distant planet from the Sun, Neptune has the strongest winds in the solar system, reaching speeds of 2,100 km/h. It's dark, cold, and whipped by supersonic winds. Neptune has 16 known moons, with Triton being the largest.

The Sun - Our Star

Facts About the Sun

The Sun is a massive ball of hot, glowing gases at the center of our solar system. It is classified as a yellow dwarf star and provides the light and heat necessary for life on Earth.

  • Diameter: 1.4 million kilometers (109 times Earth's diameter)
  • Surface temperature: About 5,500°C
  • Core temperature: About 15 million°C
  • Age: Approximately 4.6 billion years old
  • Composition: 73% hydrogen, 25% helium, 2% other elements
  • Light takes 8 minutes 20 seconds to reach Earth

Educational Resources

Enhance your understanding of the solar system with these trusted educational resources and materials.

NASA Space Place

NASA's official website for kids offers games, activities, and articles about space exploration. It features age-appropriate content with stunning images and interactive tools for learning about planets, stars, and space missions.

Books and Videos

Explore educational books and documentaries about space. Look for materials that explain complex concepts in simple terms with colorful illustrations. Videos from planetariums and science museums are excellent resources.

Planetarium Visits

Visit local planetariums for immersive experiences of the night sky. Many offer special shows for school groups that demonstrate planetary motion, constellations, and the scale of the universe in an engaging dome theater.
