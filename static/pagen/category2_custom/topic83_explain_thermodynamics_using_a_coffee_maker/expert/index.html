How a Coffee Maker Works

Heat Water

Electrical energy is used to heat clean water to the optimal brewing temperature. Maintaining the right temperature ensures proper extraction and enhances the final taste of the coffee.

Brew the Coffee

Hot water passes slowly through the ground coffee, extracting essential oils, aromas, and flavors. This brewing process determines the strength, body, and overall character of the coffee.

Collect the Coffee

The freshly brewed coffee drips into the pot below, ready to be served. Proper collection preserves heat and aroma, delivering a smooth and satisfying cup.

The Laws of Thermodynamics

1st Law of Thermodynamics

Energy is conserved

Electrical energy from the heater is converted into thermal energy that heats the water. This energy is not lost—it changes form and drives the coffee brewing process.

2nd Law of Thermodynamics

Entropy increases

Heat naturally flows from hot water to cooler coffee grounds and the surrounding environment. This irreversible process increases entropy and enables flavor extraction.

3rd Law of Thermodynamics

Absolute zero

It is impossible to reach absolute zero temperature. Even during cooling, particles retain some energy, which is why coffee never reaches a state of zero molecular motion.

0th Law of Thermodynamics

Thermal equilibrium

When the coffee, cup, and surroundings reach the same temperature, thermal equilibrium is achieved. This balance determines how long your coffee stays warm.
